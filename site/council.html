How to read this

Seats. Each electoral district (選舉區) elects several members; the candidates with the most votes win. Each voter has one vote. At every moment, the page gives each seat to whoever would win it on the votes counted so far.

Women's quota. A district electing 4 members reserves one seat for a woman, and one more for every further 4 seats (地方制度法 §33). If too few women are among the top vote-getters, the leading women outside replace the lowest-placed men. "Women's seat" marks a woman elected this way; "displaced" marks the man who loses his place.

Decided. A seat holder is marked elected when even the most damaging split of the votes still out — every remaining elector voting, all for the closest challengers — couldn't push them out. This uses electors at stations not yet counted, so it assumes nothing about turnout. With the women's quota, a man's seat counts as decided only once the quota can no longer displace him.

Map. Each county takes the color of the party holding the most seats at that moment, from light (few seats decided) to solid (all decided). Before a county's first station reports it is hatched.

Caveats. Times are when the CEC logged each station's report, for all contests at a station at once, so a council count may have been entered earlier. In 2018, ten referendums were counted the same night and counting ran past 2 a.m. in places. The replay's final state matches the CEC's elected list in every district (912 seats in 2018, 910 in 2022).
